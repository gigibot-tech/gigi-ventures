GiGi Vegan · Two levers · Projection

How far can each lever go?

Reduce demand—reduce waste—year by year.

Eating less meat acts on every animal. Wasting less food acts on the slice that is thrown away. Here the two levers get a size, a calendar and a yardstick: the goals that ProVeg, the Albert Schweitzer Foundation, the UN and the EU have set.

Try the projection ↓ Back to the totals →

01 — The total system

Everything produced, eaten and wasted.

Per region, one bar: what is consumed and what is wasted, meat weight first and animal lives second. Lever A, less demand, acts on the whole bar; lever B, less food waste, acts only on the wasted part. The detail behind each number is under “Details”; the way down into the waste figures is on GiGi Vegan.

02 — Reach

The two levers are not the same size.

Waste reduction can only ever touch the coral slice — about a fifth of all animal lives at world level. Demand reduction touches the whole bar. On the left, what each lever saves on its own; on the right, both together.

03 — Projection

Year by year: what a goal would mean in animals.

Pick a region and a goal, or set the two levers yourself. The chart shows the animals not slaughtered each year against a flat baseline: green is lever A, coral is lever B. A what-if, not a forecast — the assumptions are listed below it.

04 — Goals

Four goals, read in animal lives.

Same model, same assumptions — and a caveat for each, because none of these goals counts animal lives. Each card says what the organisation states, how we read it as a lever, and what that would mean for the world, Europe and Germany.

When a number changes
Sources & method

Numbers in brackets point to this list; marks (≠i) point to “When a number changes”. Goals are paraphrased, not quoted — read the originals.

  1. Animal lives and stage shares: Klaura et al. 2023, Leiden University — 17.97 bn animal lives lost with meat loss and waste, 2019 data; share by chain stage: production & rearing 24.9%, storage & transport 7.8%, slaughter & processing 20.0%, distribution & retail 20.6%, households & food service 26.7%.
  2. World slaughter counts: FAO (FAOSTAT), live animals slaughtered — 86.3 bn in 2024, 91% of them chickens (78.5 bn).
  3. Germany slaughter counts: Destatis press release 049/2026 (13 Feb 2026), preliminary — 48.5 m pigs, cattle, sheep, goats and horses plus 697.3 m chickens, turkeys and ducks in 2025. Destatis gives no total; the sum (≈ 746 m) is ours.
  4. Figures from our research brief that we have not re-verified: Europe-43 total ≈ 12.5 bn animals a year (2019), and the average meat per animal (chicken ≈ 1.6 kg, bovine ≈ 323 kg). Marked “Approximate” or “Brief” where they appear.
  5. ProVeg International, press release of 3 June 2024, “About ProVeg International” — proveg.org. Paraphrased here. ProVeg’s current “About us” page does not repeat the number.
  6. The earlier wording of the same goal (“50 by 40”): vegconomist interview with ProVeg.
  7. Albert Schweitzer Stiftung für unsere Mitwelt, Transparenz- und Wirkungsbericht 2023 (PDF, German): foreword, chapter 1.1 “Vision & Ansatz” — vision, mission, a three-year strategy horizon, and the aim to improve conditions for hundreds of millions of animals, mainly broiler chickens. Paraphrased.
  8. Albert Schweitzer Stiftung, “Tierprodukte befeuern die Klimakrise” (German): meat consumption in Europe would have to fall by 70–80% (attributed to the Planetary Health Diet); no target year is given.
  9. United Nations, Sustainable Development Goal 12, target 12.3: by 2030, halve per-capita food waste at retail and consumer level, and reduce food losses along production and supply chains.
  10. Directive (EU) 2025/1892 amending the Waste Framework Directive; European Commission, “Food waste reduction targets”: binding national targets for 2030 — 10% in processing and manufacturing, 30% per capita in retail and consumption together; baseline the 2021–2023 average.
  11. Household animal estimate: Meat Atlas (Fleischatlas) 2021, an advocacy source — ≈ 10.5 m land animals in household meat waste in Germany; calculation method not fully published.
  12. EU-28 meat waste: European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC), mass-flow model by product group and supply-chain stage, 2011 data — 61.7 Mt meat available, 14.2 Mt wasted (primary production 0.5, processing 2.9, retail 1.7, households 7.3, food service 1.7 Mt); fresh mass, edible and inedible parts.
  13. Germany: BZL supply balance 2025, provisional — 4.6 Mt calculated meat consumption, 54.9 kg per person (pork 28.3, poultry 14.7, beef & veal 9.7 kg). It is about 72% of the wider “meat use” of 6.37 Mt.
  14. German household waste: GfK household panel 2020 via UBA — 1.87 Mt avoidable food waste, of which 4% meat, sausage and fish (74.6 kt).
  15. Avoidable share: Thünen Report 71 — about half of household food waste is avoidable. Applied to the 2022 household total (6.3 Mt), 4% gives ≈ 125 kt, the “other basis”.
  16. Food-service meat waste: ≈ 85.8 kt (2018/19), a study estimate carried over from our earlier analysis. We could not re-verify the primary study, so it is marked unconfirmed and used only in scenario C.
  17. Our own calculation — the assumptions P1–P7 on this page, and the lever assumptions L1–L6 under the bars in The total system.
