GiGi Ventures · Concept · an idea we want to test in Berlin

It starts with a €500 grain cooker.

Free where needed—self-financing by design—vegan.

GiGi Food is an idea, not a finished plan. The question: can basic meals become infrastructure — free where needed, and paying for themselves as they grow? Our feasibility research says yes, if we begin with one cooker and let evidence decide every next step.

01 — The idea

Start with the €500 cooker. Not the €250,000 robot.

At 160 meals a day, cooking isn’t the bottleneck — serving is. A €500 grain cooker removes the cooking limit today and pays for itself in days. A €250,000 robotic kitchen would take about six years on the most optimistic numbers, and never on real costs.

“We can feed people this month for under €1,000 — and buy automation only when demand, not money or cooking, is the wall.”

The robot rung costs over 8× more than a central kitchen — and serves fewer meals.

04 — Why vegan

Every bowl is vegan.

Plants give the most nutrition per euro — and the meat system already loses an enormous amount of food and animal lives before anything reaches a plate.

05 — The pilot

365 days, clear gates.

  1. Days 1–7

    Legal-light launch

    Hygiene training for everyone (§43 IfSG, €12.50 online), food-business registration, a partner site on private land, a €300–800 kit, one rescued-food supplier.

  2. Days 8–30

    First service

    160 vegan lentil bowls a day, pay what you can, plus one €5 paying line. Every KPI measured from day one.

  3. Days 31–90

    Prove repeatability

    Found the non-profit (e.V. or gUG), get tax exemption, add a second serving point, sign the first committed paying contract.

  4. Days 91–365

    Scale only if gates pass

    A kettle and a van at 500+ meals a day. A plating rental only if volunteers become the wall. No six-figure robot.

Our gates

  • Scale to a central kitchen only if ≥500 meals/day sell out for 4+ weeks and a committed paying channel covers ≥50% of free-meal cost.
  • Consider a cooking robot only at ≥1,000 meals/day, with guaranteed paid throughput and real labour scarcity.
  • Pivot or stop if the subsidy per free meal exceeds committed funding for two months in a row.

The ask

Fund a 12-month pilot — €25,000–60,000 — not a machine.
