GiGi Ventures · Concept · Facts & report

18 billion animals. Their meat was never eaten.

Our facts—our report—where we’d anchor.

In 2019, the meat lost or wasted worldwide came from about 18 billion animals — 93.6% of them chickens. Two levers could spare them: eating less meat, or wasting less food. One acts on the whole system, the other on about a fifth of it.

Two levers

Two ways to spare the animals: eat less, or waste less.

Every animal raised for meat ends up in one of two places: eaten, or lost on the way. A lever is one thing we can change. Lever A cuts what people ask for, so fewer animals are raised at all. Lever B cuts what is thrown away, so the same meals need fewer animals. They pull at different places, and they are not the same size.

A

Lever A — eat less meat

Cuts at
The plate — what people choose to eat. Less meat wanted means fewer animals raised in the first place.
How
A plant-based meal instead of a meat one — at home, in canteens, in kitchens like GiGi Food — or a goal such as ProVeg’s “replace half of all animal products by 2040”.
Reaches
Every animal — the meat that is eaten and the meat that is wasted alike.
Germany, a year
Halve the meat eaten: ≈ 373 m of today’s 746 m animals fewer. A scenario, not a forecast.
B

Lever B — waste less food

Cuts at
Every step where meat is lost before it is eaten — five of them, from the farm to the kitchen. Share of the animal lives lost at each step, world, 2019: Production & rearing 24.9% · Storage & transport 7.8% · Slaughter & processing 20.0% · Distribution & retail 20.6% · at the plate — food service & households 26.7%, the largest step. Klaura et al. 2023
How
Better planning, storage and portion sizes; using or selling what is left over — wherever the loss happens.
Reaches
Only the lost part — about a fifth of the animal lives (≈ 22%) — so it can never do more than that.
Germany, a year
End all household meat waste: ≈ 10.5 m animals fewer — about 35 times less than halving the meat eaten. An advocacy source, households only.

The two do not simply add up: halving both saves 55.5% of the animal lives, not 61%. How far can each lever go? The yearly projection · The totals, level by level

D — Where we’d anchor

From the whole chain to one plate.

  1. 1

    The plate

    Germany eats 54.9 kg of meat per person a year — that’s the number we want to move. Every GiGi Food bowl is vegan: red lentils bring 25.4 g of protein per 100 g at €2.47/kg. GiGi Food

  2. 2

    Why chickens come first

    93.6% of the 18 billion animal lives lost are chickens. A chicken gives far less meat than a cow, so every kilo of lost chicken stands for many more lives. That’s why we count animals as well as tonnes — and why we start with plants.

  3. 3

    The last mile

    Households and food service are the largest single stage (26.7% of animal lives lost). Kitchens, canteens and everyday habits are where we’d try to act first.

Already moving

What’s been achieved for chickens so far

We looked for what organisations have actually delivered on chickens, in Germany and the EU. Two routes, and one honest gap.

Welfare route · Germany

Masthuhn-Initiative Albert Schweitzer Stiftung

The German side of the European Chicken Commitment asks food companies for better broiler welfare: space, breeding lines, stunning and slaughter. More than 110 food companies in Germany have signed up.

Report 2026 (November): of 64 companies assessed, only 3 meet all criteria. Nestlé and Rügenwalder Mühle withdrew their commitments this year.

Plant route · ProVeg

ProVeg plant-based food, not a chicken campaign

ProVeg’s documented wins are about plant-based food. In October 2020 the European Parliament rejected a ban on meat names such as “burger” and “sausage” for plant-based products.

Study with the Dutch Nutrition Center (2023): 130 plant-based meat substitutes checked. Plant-based chicken pieces and filet largely met the criteria; breaded nuggets and schnitzel did not.

The gap. As far as we could verify, ProVeg has no campaign aimed specifically at chickens; the chicken-specific pressure on companies comes from welfare groups. A plant-based bowl on the table is a third route — and that’s what GiGi Food tests.

When a number changes
Sources & method

Numbers in brackets in the totals, the drill-downs and the charts point to this list; marks (≠i) point to “When a number changes”.

  1. Animal lives and meat loss: Klaura et al. 2023, Leiden University — global model of animal lives embodied in meat loss and waste, six land-animal species, 2019 data (17.97 bn animals; 77.4 Mt carcass weight ≈ 52.4 Mt boneless edible meat, “about one sixth” of production; stage shares; Europe = 43 countries, 2.76 bn animals, 3.61 per person).
  2. EU-28 meat waste: European Commission Joint Research Centre (JRC), mass-flow model by product group and supply-chain stage, 2011 data — 61.7 Mt meat available, 14.2 Mt wasted (primary production 0.5, processing 2.9, retail 1.7, households 7.3, food service 1.7 Mt); fresh mass, edible and inedible parts.
  3. World food waste: UNEP Food Waste Index Report 2024 — 1.05 bn t in 2022 (households 631 Mt, food service 290 Mt, retail 131 Mt; 132 kg per person). Waste only: losses on farms and in processing are not included. The 1.05 bn t is 19% of the food available to consumers (retail, food service and households); dividing the two gives ≈ 5.5 bn t, our derived total of food available. The 2021 report counted 931 Mt = 17% of the food available (2019) — the same order of magnitude.
  4. EU and Germany food waste: Eurostat, food waste by sector (env_wasfw), extracted November 2026. 2022 as revised: EU-27 57.5 Mt (households 30.5 Mt); Germany 10.8 Mt (households 6.3 Mt, food service 2.0 Mt — a 76/24 split at the consumption stage). Latest: EU-27 57.9 Mt in 2024 (released 29 September 2026); for Germany and Romania the latest year is 2023.
  5. Chickens slaughtered: Eurostat, slaughtering in slaughterhouses (apro_mt_pann), 2022, number of animals. Sum of reporting EU countries ≈ 6.16 bn; Austria, Estonia, Luxembourg and Slovakia don’t publish it.
  6. World meat consumption: OECD-FAO Agricultural Outlook 2025–2034 — 29.3 kg per person (retail weight) projected for 2034, 0.9 kg above the base period; ≈ 28.4 kg is our derivation. The Outlook counts consumption as food availability, which does not account for waste.
  7. World meat production: FAO estimate for 2024 — about 374 Mt carcass weight; poultry, at about 150 Mt, is now the largest meat.
  8. EU meat consumption: European Commission, EU agricultural outlook — 66.6 kg per person (retail weight) and 37.47 Mt (carcass weight), average 2022–24. Different weight bases: the two are never multiplied.
  9. Germany: BZL supply balance 2025, provisional — 4.6 Mt calculated meat consumption, 54.9 kg per person (pork 28.3, poultry 14.7, beef & veal 9.7 kg). It is about 72% of the wider “meat use” of 6.37 Mt.
  10. German household waste: GfK household panel 2020 via UBA — 1.87 Mt avoidable food waste, of which 4% meat, sausage and fish (74.6 kt).
  11. Avoidable share: Thünen Report 71 — about half of household food waste is avoidable. Applied to the 2022 household total (6.3 Mt), 4% gives ≈ 125 kt, the “other basis”.
  12. Household animal estimate: Meat Atlas (Fleischatlas) 2021, an NGO model — ≈ 10.5 m land animals, 8.9 m of them chickens; calculation method not fully published, used only as a scenario input.
  13. Food-service meat waste: ≈ 85.8 kt (2018/19), a study estimate carried over from our earlier analysis. We could not re-verify the primary study, so it is marked unconfirmed and used only in scenario C.
  14. Our model: the lever assumptions L1–L6 (see The total system), scenarios A, B and C and the assumptions A1–A6 — see Germany, two paths.
  15. World slaughter counts: FAO (FAOSTAT), live animals slaughtered — 86.3 bn in 2024, 91% of them chickens (78.5 bn). The “≈ 80 bn” for 2019 is a rounded figure; Our World in Data reports 83 bn for 2022. Counts exclude animals that die before slaughter and male chicks culled in egg production.
  16. Germany slaughter counts: Destatis press release 049/2026 (13 Feb 2026), preliminary — 48.5 m pigs, cattle, sheep, goats and horses plus 697.3 m chickens, turkeys and ducks in 2025. Destatis gives no total; the sum (≈ 746 m) is ours.
  17. Figures from our research brief that we have not re-verified: world meat production 335 Mt (2019), Europe-43 total ≈ 12.5 bn animals, EU-27 poultry ≈ 6.4 bn (2020), average meat per animal (chicken ≈ 1.6 kg, bovine ≈ 323 kg). Marked “Brief” or “Approximate” where they appear.
  18. Food lost before the shop: FAO, “Food loss and food waste” — 13.2% of food is lost in the supply chain after harvest on farms and before the retail stage (the UN News report on UNEP’s index says “around 13 per cent”). UNEP’s waste figures start at retail, so the two do not overlap — but they are different models and are never added up.
  • Chickens: Albert Schweitzer Stiftung, Masthuhn-Report 2025 (more than 110 companies in Germany) and Masthuhn-Report 2026 (64 companies assessed), reported by vegconomist, November 2026. ProVeg: European Parliament vote of 23 October 2020 on plant-based naming; ProVeg Netherlands × Netherlands Nutrition Center, 7 September 2023.
  • These models use different years, regions and weight definitions (carcass, retail, boneless). They are not directly comparable and are never added up or divided by each other.
